Exploring the curricular frameworks and documents that guide education in Uruguay.

01

Curricular framework

Main Curricular Documents

ANEP establishes a set of curricular documents that guide the organisation of teaching, expected learning outcomes and pedagogical practices across different educational levels.

These documents have different levels of specificity and are connected to ensure coherence and continuity throughout students' educational trajectories.

The curriculum is not simply a selection of contents. It also reflects educational, cultural and pedagogical decisions about which knowledge is considered relevant and which abilities students are expected to develop.

For teachers, these documents provide a framework for planning lessons, selecting teaching strategies and assessing learning.

National Curriculum Framework (Marco Curricular Nacional — MCN)

The MCN is the broadest document within ANEP's curricular structure. It establishes the system's guiding principles, ten general competencies and the expected graduation profile at the end of compulsory education.

The ten general competencies are:

  1. Communication.
  2. Creative thinking.
  3. Critical thinking.
  4. Scientific thinking.
  5. Computational thinking.
  6. Metacognitive competence.
  7. Intrapersonal competence.
  8. Initiative and action orientation.
  9. Relationships with others.
  10. Local, global and digital citizenship.

These competencies aim to promote comprehensive education by connecting knowledge, skills, attitudes and values.

Learning Progressions (Progresiones de aprendizaje)

Learning progressions describe how students develop competencies throughout their educational trajectories.

They help identify different levels of progress and increasing complexity, recognising that students do not all learn at the same pace or in exactly the same way.

They are also important references for formative assessment and pedagogical decision-making.

Stage Profiles (Perfiles de tramo)

Stage profiles establish the expected achievements at the end of each tramo of Integrated Basic Education.

They help identify the learning students are expected to have developed and support continuity between educational levels.

Integrated Basic Education (Educación Básica Integrada — EBI)

EBI organises compulsory education from age three in Early Childhood Education through ninth grade. It is structured into three cycles and six tramos:

Tramo Educational levels
1 Initial 3, 4 and 5 years
2 1st and 2nd grades
3 3rd and 4th grades
4 5th and 6th grades
5 7th and 8th grades
6 9th grade

This organisation aims to strengthen continuity and reduce unnecessary fragmentation between educational stages.

Curricular Programmes of the Unidades Curriculares

Curricular programmes are the most specific documents within the curricular structure. They include specific competencies, contents, teaching guidelines and achievement criteria.

Their purpose is to guide lesson planning while allowing teachers to connect general curricular guidelines with students' characteristics and the particularities of each educational context.

ANEP makes these documents available through its official curricular documents portal.

02

Subject area

English Language Teaching within the Curriculum

English teaching contributes to the development of students' communicative competencies and their overall education.

Within Integrated Basic Education, the foreign language curriculum focuses on progressively developing students' ability to understand and produce messages in different communicative situations.

In lower secondary education, English is included as a specific unidad curricular.

From a teaching perspective, language learning involves four fundamental skills:

Skill Expected learning
Listening Understanding messages, conversations and instructions.
Speaking Participating in interactions and communicating ideas.
Reading Interpreting texts from different genres and formats.
Writing Producing texts for different purposes and audiences.

These skills should be developed in an integrated and progressive way through meaningful activities that allow students to use the language in real or simulated contexts.

Learning English also involves approaching other cultures, recognising linguistic diversity and developing respect for different forms of communication.

03

Learning goals

Competencies and Expected Learning Outcomes across Cycles

The organisation into cycles and tramos makes it possible to understand learning as a progressive process.

In English teaching, expected outcomes should be interpreted according to the corresponding official programmes and stage profiles. It is important not to assign specific language proficiency levels unless they are explicitly established in the curricular documents.

In general terms, the main learning emphases can be summarised as follows:

Cycle Educational trajectory General emphasis in English
First cycle Early Childhood Education to 2nd grade Initial contact with the language, familiarisation with sounds, words, expressions and everyday situations.
Second cycle 3rd to 6th grade Vocabulary development, understanding messages, participating in simple interactions and approaching short texts.
Third cycle 7th to 9th grade Greater communicative autonomy, understanding more complex texts and producing spoken and written messages for different purposes.

This table provides a general overview rather than a replacement for the specific learning outcomes established in official programmes.

Upper Secondary Education and Learning Continuity

English learning continues throughout Upper Secondary Education through the curricular proposals of both secondary and technical-professional education.

At this level, the language becomes particularly relevant to further education and employment.

Programmes related to technology, tourism, administration and services highlight the importance of English in different professional areas. Developing specific communicative skills can help students access specialised information, interact with speakers of other languages and participate in increasingly international work environments.

Continuity between Integrated Basic Education and Upper Secondary Education is essential to avoid interruptions in students' language learning.

04

Teaching practice

Teaching Approaches and Assessment

The curricular approach promotes student-centred teaching that takes into account learners' previous knowledge, interests, needs and different learning paces.

In English teaching, this means designing activities that allow students to use the language for concrete communicative purposes.

Possible teaching strategies include:

  • Language games and interactive activities.
  • Songs, stories and dramatizations.
  • Oral exchanges and role-playing.
  • Interdisciplinary projects.
  • Audiovisual and digital resources.

Assessment is understood as a continuous and formative process. Its purpose is to collect evidence of learning, identify students' progress and provide feedback that helps them improve.

Competencies, learning progressions and achievement criteria can guide teachers' decisions and help them adapt their proposals to students' needs.

05

Reflection

Implications for Teacher Education

Understanding curricular documents is an essential part of professional teacher education.

For future English teachers, interpreting the MCN, learning progressions and specific curricular programmes means understanding which learning outcomes should be developed, how they are organised throughout students' educational trajectories and which teaching strategies may support them.

Lesson planning requires coherent connections between competencies, contents, learning goals, teaching strategies and assessment criteria.

Teachers must also consider students' sociocultural backgrounds and the characteristics of each institution, avoiding the use of teaching proposals that are disconnected from their contexts.

Therefore, professional teacher education involves more than disciplinary knowledge. It also requires developing the ability to reflect on, investigate, plan and assess educational practices.

Considerations

The analysis of the Uruguayan education system shows that it is a complex institutional structure shaped by historical principles and successive transformations intended to respond to society's educational needs.

Its different levels and teaching modalities, together with the diversity of public and private institutions, create a system that seeks to guarantee access to education and support continuity throughout students' educational trajectories.

In Soriano, this structure has particular characteristics due to the geographical distribution of educational institutions and the department's social and productive context. The presence of urban and rural schools, secondary schools, UTU centres, private institutions and teacher education centres provides different opportunities, while also creating challenges related to accessibility and educational continuity.

English teaching is an important part of this reality. Public programmes, private schools and language institutes provide different opportunities for students to learn the language. However, access alone is not enough: students also need sustained learning experiences, appropriately trained teachers and teaching proposals that respond to the diversity of educational contexts.

The local educational field also offers different possible professional pathways for future English teachers. Public education, private schools and language institutes provide distinct working environments, while professional organisations such as AUDEPPi and URUTESOL can contribute to continued development and exchange with other teachers.

From the perspective of teacher education, understanding ANEP's curricular documents is essential for recognising the principles that guide educational practice and the responsibility involved in turning those guidelines into meaningful learning experiences.

Ultimately, strengthening education in Soriano requires connecting national policies with the characteristics of the department. For future teachers, understanding this context is an important part of preparing to work in the profession and contribute to the educational community.
