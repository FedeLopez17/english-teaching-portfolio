BECOMING A TEACHER / PORTFOLIO

Educational
Agents.

Interviews and conversations with people connected to education.

01

Interviews

Learning from experience

The purpose of these interviews was to learn more about the teaching profession through the experiences of people who are already working or have been involved in education. I wanted to go beyond what we discuss in teacher education and get a better idea of what teaching actually looks like in different contexts.

I chose participants with different roles and levels of experience, as I felt each of them could offer a valuable perspective. The interviews were conducted through email and WhatsApp. I prepared a set of questions for each participant, although I made it clear that they didn't have to answer all of them. Instead, I encouraged them to choose the questions that resonated with them the most and elaborate on their answers as much or as little as they wanted. They could respond in either Spanish or English, either in writing or through audio recordings.

The only question I asked everyone was the one inviting them to introduce themselves. Some questions were shared between participants when I wanted to compare their perspectives, but they were also free to bring up anything else they felt was worth sharing, even if I hadn't asked about it directly.

The interviews are presented individually, organised by participant rather than by question or topic. This means they differ in length and level of detail, depending on how much each person chose to share. I decided to preserve these differences instead of making all the interviews follow exactly the same structure.

While some other sections of this portfolio have been translated into English, I decided to keep the interviews in their original language. The only changes made are related to transcription and formatting. Audio recordings have been transcribed, unnecessary fillers (such as repeated "uhms") have been removed, and the formatting has been standardised to make the answers easier to read. Other than these minor adjustments, the responses have not been rewritten or shortened, and are presented as close as possible to the interviewees' original words.

01 Heather Seltzer English teacher · Online language institute director +

1. Could you briefly introduce yourself and tell us a little about your professional journey as an English teacher?

My name is Heather Seltzer, and I’m originally from the United States. I currently live in Argentina, where I teach online, specialising in one-to-one Business English and advanced English. I also run my own online language institute, where we offer courses from A1 to C2 (Proficiency).

My professional background was originally in business management, but I was doing some volunteer teaching on the side. I eventually decided to give English teaching a try for a year, and I started by earning my Cambridge CELTA qualification. Later, I completed DELTA Modules 1 and 2.

Over the course of my career, I became Director of Studies at an International House school, where I was involved in syllabus development, course design, and teacher training.

2. What initially motivated you to become an English teacher?

I was volunteering in my spare time, teaching literacy to adults in the United States, and I found so much joy in the experience that I decided to take a sabbatical from my career in business management and try teaching English overseas.

What was originally meant to be a temporary experience ended up becoming my true calling. I discovered that helping students achieve their goals brought me far more satisfaction and fulfilment than I had ever found working in business.

3. Have academic events such as conferences, seminars or festivals influenced your teaching?

When it comes to continuing professional development, I’ve found enormous value in completing my CELTA and DELTA qualifications. I was also fortunate enough to be part of the International House organisation for many years, where I benefited from ongoing professional development, including classroom observations, feedback, and training sessions.

While I haven’t had the opportunity to attend many conferences, it’s certainly something I’d like to do more of in the future. I believe that continuing to learn and develop as a teacher is essential, regardless of how much experience you have.

4. What skills do you think future English teachers should develop?

I think future English teachers should first develop the ability to create a classroom environment that fosters confidence and trust, not only between the teacher and students, but also among classmates. Building rapport, creating a positive atmosphere, and making students feel comfortable enough to take risks and make mistakes are essential. Without that foundation, I don’t think students can truly make progress.

Secondly, I think teachers should develop the skills to foster a genuine love of reading. We have a wonderful opportunity, even when teaching English as a second language, to engage students with authentic materials such as fiction, articles, and other meaningful texts. It’s about encouraging curiosity, thoughtful discussion, and a deeper connection with what students read, rather than simply completing comprehension exercises.

5. How important are collaboration and professional networks in teaching?

I think collaboration and professional networks are absolutely essential in teaching. I was part of the International House community for over eight years, and while being part of a global organisation was incredibly valuable, I think what made the biggest difference was the everyday collaboration in the teachers’ room.

The exchange of tips and tricks, the support from colleagues, helping each other with marking, sharing resources, and simply being able to discuss the challenges of teaching were invaluable experiences. That environment really helped shape me into the teacher I am today.

Fortunately, I’ve built strong professional relationships over the years, so even though I now work entirely online, I can still tap into that wealth of knowledge, experience, and resources. Having said that, it’s not quite the same as being face-to-face with colleagues and having those spontaneous conversations in the teachers’ room.

6. What has been the most rewarding aspect of your teaching career so far?

By far, the most rewarding aspect of my teaching career has been seeing students achieve very specific, often challenging goals. Whether it’s landing their dream job overseas, getting accepted into a university, or something more personal, like finally feeling confident enough to travel without worrying about the language barrier, those moments are incredibly fulfilling.

There’s something really special about watching someone grow in confidence, find their voice in another language, and then seeing the tangible results of that growth. Knowing that learning English has genuinely changed someone’s life for the better is what makes teaching so rewarding for me.

7. What are some of the biggest challenges you face as an English teacher?

One of my biggest concerns is what I see as a growing gap in students’ reading comprehension and writing fluency. While the highest-achieving students continue to impress, I feel that many students in the middle are falling further behind, partly due to changing priorities in schools and at home.

This creates a particular challenge in language teaching because literacy skills are transferable. Increasingly, we encounter students who struggle with tasks in English that they may not yet be able to perform confidently in their first language.

For me, the challenge is recognising when the difficulty goes beyond English itself and finding ways to support those underlying skills. Ideally, what students develop in our classrooms should benefit them not only in English but in their first language as well.

8. How do you adapt your teaching methods to meet the different needs and learning styles of your students?

For me, it starts with planning. I’ve been fortunate enough to receive training in several different teaching methodologies, which has given me the flexibility to adapt my approach depending on the students I’m working with.

I create individual profiles for my students and classes, taking into account their current level, motivations, needs, and interests. I then use these profiles to develop materials and plan lessons that are relevant and engaging for them.

I try to vary my teaching approaches, although I tend to lean towards communicative and task-based learning. I also use techniques like Test-Teach-Test, particularly when I want students to see immediate progress, which can be a great confidence booster.

I’m constantly adapting my lesson planning based on what students need, what they’ve already covered, and where their strengths and weaknesses lie. AI has made this process considerably easier, allowing me to maintain detailed learner profiles and track individual progress. This means I can create much more personalised materials and ensure that my lessons are genuinely tailored to the students in front of me.

9. What role do you think technology and artificial intelligence should play in English language teaching?

As I mentioned earlier, I think AI can be an incredibly valuable tool for teachers, provided they already have a solid understanding of what makes an effective lesson. With the right pedagogical knowledge, teachers can use AI to create engaging, personalised materials and lesson plans that respond to their students’ specific needs.

I also think we’re on the cusp of a major shift, both in the English language teaching market and in the development of new technologies that will increasingly compete with what we consider traditional English teaching.

What gives me some reassurance, though, is that human interaction and face-to-face communication remain such fundamental parts of who we are. I believe students will continue to value the personal connection, warmth, and rapport that a teacher brings to the classroom. There are certain communication skills, particularly those involving confidence, spontaneity, and genuine human connection, that I think are much harder to develop through an AI-powered platform alone.

Ultimately, I see AI as a powerful tool to support and enhance teaching, rather than something that should replace the human relationships at the heart of learning.

02 Noel Cabrera English teacher · IFD student · Secondary education +

1. Could you briefly introduce yourself and tell us a little about your professional journey as an English teacher?

My name is Noel Cabrera, and I've been teaching English for over six years, mainly in private language institutes. I'm currently studying at IFD Mercedes, and I also have experience teaching in secondary schools. Throughout my career, I've had the opportunity to work with students from different backgrounds and age groups. Although I enjoy working with all kinds of learners, I particularly enjoy teaching teenagers and young adults.

2. What does being a professional English teacher mean to you?

Being a professional English teacher means taking on a lot of responsibilities and challenges. It requires hard work, commitment and continuous learning. I had to study a lot to get to where I am today, and I've had to overcome many difficulties along the way. I think one of the most important things is understanding that you never stop learning. Sometimes you feel that you're not capable of doing something, but if you keep trying and doing your best, you can overcome those difficulties and become a better teacher.

3. What professional activities do you engage in beyond classroom teaching?

I try to stay up to date and continuously reflect on my own teaching through different professional activities, such as conferences and seminars. Whenever these opportunities arise, I try to make the most of them and use what I learn to improve my teaching practice. I believe these activities are important because they allow me to exchange ideas with other teachers and discover new approaches that I can eventually bring into the classroom.

4. How important is continuous professional development in your career?

Continuous professional development is very important because you need to stay up to date, since things are constantly changing in education. There are different educational trends nowadays, and you should try to adapt them to your students' needs in order to keep them engaged. You need to try different approaches, and for that, you need the right tools. Attending seminars, conferences and courses is a great way to acquire those tools and continue improving as a teacher.

5. Have academic events such as conferences, seminars or festivals influenced your teaching?

Yes, they have been excellent tools for improving my teaching practice. As I mentioned before, staying up to date with educational developments is very important because students' needs are constantly changing. Therefore, the methods we use to reach them and engage them should change as well. These events have given me the opportunity to learn about different approaches and reflect on how I can apply them in my own classes.

6. How do you keep learning about English language teaching?

Whenever a new course becomes available, I try to take part in it so that I can stay informed and incorporate new teaching concepts into my practice, or refresh my knowledge of existing ones. I think it is important to be open to new ideas and resources. The most recent courses I attended, particularly those about AI and its use in the classroom, were very useful because they introduced me to tools that can make teaching and learning more effective.

7. What skills do you think future English teachers should develop?

Above all, I think they should develop the ability to adapt to their students' needs. Every group is different, and what works well with one group might not work with another. Although traditional teaching methods will always have their place, it is essential to introduce new resources and approaches to help students learn more effectively. I would also say that patience and flexibility are important, since teaching doesn't always go according to plan.

8. What advice would you give to someone who is beginning their teacher education?

Be aware of your own limitations. We are not perfect, and there will always be things we don't know or situations we don't know how to handle. However, we should never stop learning and trying to improve. Don't get discouraged when things don't go as expected. Experience is something you build over time, so keep practising and doing your best. After all, practice makes perfect.

9. What is something about the teaching profession that you wish you had known when you were a student teacher?

I think it is important to teach within a context. What I mean is that you should follow a particular topic throughout your lessons, such as health, transport or social media, rather than constantly jumping from one topic to another just to introduce a grammar point. Otherwise, students might get confused or fail to see the connection between what they are learning. I believe lessons work better when students can connect different language points to a broader topic.

10. How important are collaboration and professional networks in teaching?

They're really important. Sharing ideas with other colleagues can help you in many ways. Not only do you feel supported throughout the process, but you also get new ideas that you can put into practice in the classroom. At the same time, you can share resources and learn different ways of dealing with challenges that may arise. I think having colleagues you can turn to is particularly valuable because teaching can sometimes be quite challenging, and you don't always have all the answers yourself.

11. How has your experience teaching at Anglo been?

It's been a valuable experience. Although the teaching approach is somewhat traditional, I work with small groups, which has given me the opportunity to get to know different learning styles and personalities. This has helped me understand that students don't all learn in the same way, so I've had to develop different strategies to manage those differences in the classroom. Overall, I'd say it has been a very worthwhile experience because it has helped me grow professionally.

12. How has your experience teaching in high schools been?

It's been a rewarding experience. At the beginning, I thought it would be very difficult to get students' attention, but that hasn't really been the case. I've realized that if you're well prepared and know how to adapt to different situations, things tend to flow quite naturally. Of course, there are obstacles along the way, and every group has its own challenges, but I've learned a lot from those experiences. Overall, it's been a really positive experience.

13. What are the main differences between the two?

Well, one of the main differences is the size of the groups. Public high school classes are generally larger than those at private institutes.

Another difference is students' motivation. At public high schools, some students aren't necessarily interested in learning English because they see it as just another subject they have to pass. Something similar can happen at private institutes, but in general, students there are more interested in learning the language. So, at high schools, teachers often have to make a greater effort to motivate students and catch their attention.

I'd also say that teaching at high schools requires more preparation from the teacher. At a private institute like Anglo, most of the resources are already available, whereas at high schools, you often have to prepare your own materials. You might have a textbook, but if you want to use additional resources, you may have to find or create them yourself. This requires more time and effort from the teacher, but it also gives you more opportunities to be creative and adapt your lessons to your students' needs.

03 Laura Silvera English teacher · 13 years of experience +

1. Could you briefly introduce yourself and tell us a little about your professional journey as an English teacher?

I'm an English teacher, and I've been teaching for 13 years. Throughout my career, I've worked with students at different levels and in different educational programmes, which has given me the opportunity to gain a wide range of experiences and continue growing both personally and professionally.

2. What initially motivated you to become an English teacher?

Initially, I was motivated by how easily I had always been able to learn the language. Besides that, I saw teaching as an accessible career opportunity and a good way to develop professionally.

3. What does being a professional English teacher mean to you?

After 13 years of teaching, my perspective has changed considerably. Today, I understand the true value of professional training and the importance of having the right pedagogical and teaching tools. My education prepared me to deal with situations that I probably hadn't even imagined at the beginning, and it helped me develop a much more conscious and reflective approach to my teaching practice.

4. What advice would you give to someone who is beginning their teacher education?

I'd advise them to make the most of every learning opportunity during their training. Sometimes, we aren't fully aware of how much we're going to need those tools in the future. Everything we learn eventually becomes valuable and helps us face the realities of the classroom with greater confidence and preparation.

5. What has been the most rewarding aspect of your teaching career so far?

One of the most rewarding aspects has been sharing my educational journey with fellow students, with whom I've learned to overcome obstacles and challenges together. Those experiences create bonds that go far beyond academics. In many cases, the people who were once our classmates end up becoming lifelong companions.

6. What are some of the biggest challenges you face as an English teacher?

One of the biggest challenges is addressing the different learning difficulties and paces that coexist within the same group. Another important challenge has been maintaining continuous professional development, always looking for ways to stay up to date and adapt to new teaching approaches and methodologies.

7. How has your teaching approach changed since you started your career?

Over the years, I've learned to manage time differently, both my own time when teaching and my students' time when learning. Nowadays, I prioritise meaningful learning opportunities, even when that means making changes to what I've planned. I also try to make language learning more authentic, practical and meaningful for students, so they can actually use the language in real-life situations.

8. Is there anything you would like to change about the education system regarding English language teaching?

I'd like to see a greater emphasis on the communicative use of English and better resources in educational institutions to develop speaking and conversation skills. I also think it would be very beneficial to have native speakers involved in the learning process, allowing students to put what they've learned into practice through real interactions.

04 Rita Bardas English teacher · Public primary and secondary education +

1. Could you briefly introduce yourself and tell us a little about your professional journey as an English teacher?

My name is Rita Bardas, and I've been working as an English teacher since 1994, mainly in public primary and secondary schools in Uruguay. Throughout my career, I've had the opportunity to work in different educational contexts and take part in innovative experiences in foreign language teaching, both inside and outside the classroom. These experiences have allowed me to grow professionally and explore different approaches to language education.

2. What initially motivated you to become an English teacher?

My initial motivation was the opportunity to teach a foreign language that I was passionate about. During the 1990s, a new English teaching programme was introduced in public schools, offering support to teachers, training courses, teaching resources and seminars with international speakers. There were also opportunities to meet teachers from all over the country and exchange experiences. All of this enriched my teaching practice and strengthened my desire to continue working as an English teacher. That first experience as a new teacher also inspired me to pursue further professional development through both public and private certifications.

3. What are some of the biggest challenges English teachers face nowadays?

English teachers face several challenges. One of them is working with a large number of groups and students, often with very different previous experiences in learning a foreign language. In many educational contexts, English isn't part of students' everyday lives, so they sometimes see it as something distant or lacking practical value.

These differences make classroom management more challenging. Teachers need to create or adapt materials to suit the specific needs and realities of each group. In other words, we need to develop a more contextualised approach to teaching, taking students' backgrounds and circumstances into account.

4. How relevant is English language education for Uruguayan children and teenagers?

I believe English education is essential for children and young people. This is also recognised by Uruguayan educational legislation, particularly the General Education Law (18.437), and by the goal of making Uruguay a multilingual country.

English allows students to access up-to-date information and is the main language used in international scientific communication. Learning another language also brings cognitive benefits, such as developing metacognitive skills and reflecting on one's own language. It allows students to organise their thoughts in new ways and gain a better understanding of different perspectives and ways of seeing the world. For all these reasons, I consider introducing English at an early age essential.

5. From your perspective, how would you describe the education students receive in primary and secondary school?

I don't think it's possible to give a single answer because there are different approaches to English language education in Uruguay. For example, we have rural primary education, the Inglés sin Límites programme, remote English lessons through Ceibal, face-to-face teaching in public schools and different programmes in secondary education.

Overall, I have a very positive view of the progress made in recent years. English language education has received increasing attention, and there are teachers throughout the country carrying out successful and innovative projects. English teaching has also become widely available from fourth grade onwards, through different formats.

Although there may be some exceptions, most children reach secondary school with some previous knowledge of English. However, their proficiency varies depending on how many years they've been exposed to the language and the type of instruction they've received.

6. Do you think the level of English students develop throughout their education is satisfactory?

Ideally, students should reach a B2 level according to the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages by the time they finish upper secondary education.

Many students manage to achieve this after several years of exposure to the language. There are also opportunities for students in public secondary education to prepare for and take international examinations free of charge through the Language Policies programme, provided they meet the academic requirements, including obtaining a grade of 8.

However, the challenges I mentioned earlier, the different learning contexts and the varying backgrounds of English teachers all contribute to differences in students' language development. I believe there is still a lot of work to be done if we want to achieve the goal of making Uruguay a multilingual country by 2030.

7. Is there anything you would like to change about the education system regarding English language teaching?

I think there should be better coordination between English teaching in primary and secondary education. Sometimes, students who have been learning English since preschool and have reached an A2 level by the end of sixth grade seem to lose some of that progress when they enter seventh grade.

I understand that this isn't easy to address because English is taught through different programmes and formats in public primary schools. However, improving coordination between the two educational levels would help ensure a smoother transition and greater continuity in students' learning.

8. How important is continuous professional development in your career?

Continuous professional development (CPD) is essential because it has a direct impact on students' learning. It provides teachers with the tools they need to face classroom challenges, make informed decisions and critically reflect on their own teaching practices.

It also creates opportunities for professional discussion and collaboration with colleagues, helping teachers avoid the isolation that can sometimes lead to exhaustion or burnout.

Personally, I really enjoy professional development opportunities because they expose me to different perspectives and allow me to look at my teaching practice from a fresh point of view. I believe that continuing to learn is an essential part of being a good teacher.

9. What advice would you give to someone who is beginning their teacher education?

I'd advise them not to work in isolation and to build a professional network with other teachers. Joining local groups, WhatsApp communities and other professional networks can help them stay informed about scholarships, training opportunities and other resources.

I'd also encourage them to continue learning through reading, courses and different educational programmes, both nationally and internationally. Nowadays, there are many free opportunities available, including face-to-face courses, online courses and self-paced programmes that allow you to learn from almost anywhere and at any time.

Another important piece of advice is not to take students' comments personally, as this can help prevent unnecessary conflicts in the classroom. It's also important to find ways to recharge outside work, spending time with friends and family or enjoying activities such as listening to music.

Finally, I'd say that they should try to remain positive, even though they will inevitably encounter people with negative views about the teaching profession. Everyone's experience is different. Personally, I find teaching a truly fascinating profession because no two days are the same. It requires commitment, creativity and flexibility to deal with the different situations that arise, but that's also what makes it so rewarding.

05 María Alberro Primary teacher · Didactics teacher · Educational leadership +

1. ¿Podrías presentarte brevemente y contarnos un poco sobre su trayectoria profesional?

Soy María Alberro, docente con 25 años de trayectoria. Durante 17 años me he dedicado a la enseñanza desde un doble rol: la formación de alumnos escolares y de estudiantes magisteriales. De esos 17 años, 12 transcurrieron directamente en el aula y los últimos 5 en la gestión de un Centro Educativo de Práctica, con formación específica en Didáctica.

También trabajé durante 14 años en un cargo de docencia indirecta en el Instituto de Formación Docente, articulando y apoyando, como Docente Orientadora, a los estudiantes de las distintas carreras que la institución ofrecía en aquel momento. En los últimos años me he desempeñado como profesora de Didáctica 1 y 2 en la formación de maestros.

2. ¿Qué importancia atribuyes actualmente a la enseñanza del inglés en la formación integral de los estudiantes de Educación Primaria?

Le atribuyo una importancia considerable porque contribuye al desarrollo de las habilidades comunicativas de los alumnos. No se trata solamente de aprender otro idioma, sino también de acercarse a otras culturas y desarrollar distintas capacidades, ampliando sus horizontes, por decirlo de alguna manera.

A nivel de las políticas educativas, se le ha otorgado un lugar bastante privilegiado a la enseñanza de otros idiomas, no solamente del inglés, sino también de una segunda lengua. Sin lugar a dudas, se entiende que, desde el punto de vista pedagógico, esta formación realiza aportes muy valiosos al desarrollo integral de los niños.

3. ¿Cómo entiendes el rol de la dirección en la articulación de los planes de estudio de inglés con la realidad pedagógica e institucional de la escuela?

Toda escuela tiene un Proyecto Institucional orientado a la formación integral de los alumnos. Creo que es allí donde el director debe incluir la formación en inglés.

A veces, cuando se cuenta con un profesor presencial, resulta más fácil implementarlo, ya que es un docente más de la institución y, al estar presente, conoce su realidad. Esto le permite adaptar el programa al Proyecto Institucional de la escuela y a las necesidades de los estudiantes.

Cuando el profesor es remoto, la situación es mucho más difícil. En realidad, el Programa de Políticas Lingüísticas deriva la propuesta a los institutos que cuentan con los docentes remotos. En ese caso, la articulación se realiza principalmente con el maestro de clase, que es quien dispone de horas remuneradas para coordinar.

Por eso, a veces el Proyecto Institucional de la escuela avanza en una dirección y la enseñanza del inglés no logra acompañarlo.

4. ¿Qué valoración realizas de las modalidades presencial y remota de enseñanza del inglés?

Mi valoración de ambas modalidades es positiva, aunque las experiencias son diferentes.

El profesor de inglés que trabaja presencialmente en la escuela comprende la realidad de la institución y es un docente más dentro de ella. Complementa el trabajo que se viene realizando y contribuye a mantener una mayor coherencia institucional. Al conocer el contexto, puede desarrollar una enseñanza más situada, lo que favorece el vínculo con los alumnos. También tiene una observación más directa de cómo aprenden, puede atender mejor sus necesidades particulares y dar continuidad al proceso de aprendizaje.

El profesor remoto, en cambio, trabaja durante 45 minutos una vez por semana y, a veces, ni siquiera pertenece al país, sino que trabaja desde otros lugares. En algunas ocasiones, su formación proviene de institutos privados; es decir, no necesariamente cuenta con una formación docente equivalente ni sigue una misma orientación pedagógica. Recibe las orientaciones de su instituto y coordina con el maestro de clase.

Se supone que el maestro de clase debe desarrollar dos instancias semanales de enseñanza presencial, apoyándose en los materiales proporcionados por el profesor remoto. Sin embargo, esto depende de la formación del maestro y de su disposición para trabajar con el inglés.

Por eso, considero que la experiencia de aprendizaje que reciben los alumnos es diferente en ambas modalidades.

5. ¿Cuáles consideras que son los principales desafíos que enfrenta actualmente la enseñanza del inglés en Educación Primaria y qué aspectos de las propuestas vigentes deberían fortalecerse?

Los desafíos son muchos. Uno de ellos es la formación de los docentes. Otro tiene que ver con los recursos tecnológicos disponibles en las escuelas, ya que a veces las salas de videoconferencia están rotas o presentan problemas de audio y conectividad. En otras ocasiones, los alumnos no cuentan con suficientes dispositivos para trabajar.

Otro desafío, como ya mencioné, es lograr una articulación entre el Proyecto Institucional, el Programa de Inglés y las necesidades educativas particulares de los niños.

Siempre hay que fortalecer la coordinación entre profesores y maestros, la evaluación continua y el uso pedagógico de los recursos. A veces contamos con recursos excelentes, pero no sabemos cómo utilizarlos o no existe la disposición necesaria para hacerlo.

6. Sabiendo que existe una formación de inglés en Ceibal para maestros, desde tu punto de vista, ¿es imprescindible para poder ocupar cargos vinculados con el inglés remoto?

La pregunta es muy interesante. Toda formación es valiosa y bienvenida. Se supone que el docente de aula cuenta con la experiencia didáctica necesaria para trabajar con los alumnos de la escuela, pero quizás no tiene la formación disciplinar ni algunas de las didácticas específicas que requiere la enseñanza de una lengua extranjera.

Sin embargo, es una situación difícil porque esta formación es voluntaria. Los cursos que ofrece Ceibal siempre son buenos; algunos son presenciales y otros a distancia, pero dependen de la voluntad del docente, se realizan fuera del horario laboral y no son remunerados.

Por lo tanto, esto también representa un desafío. Tampoco existe un incentivo económico significativo, ya que al docente se le pagan $1000 por coordinar con un profesor remoto. Esto no constituye un estímulo demasiado importante para que los maestros inviertan tiempo en su formación.

7. ¿Existe actualmente algún mecanismo de registro y seguimiento para conocer los avances de los grupos que participan en Ceibal en Inglés?

En mi caso, como hay muchas actividades y muchos grupos, las instancias de inglés no pueden superponerse con otras actividades ni con la práctica docente. Por lo tanto, existe algún tipo de registro y seguimiento de la continuidad del programa.

En cuanto a los contenidos, es el instituto al que pertenece el profesor el que realiza la selección. Luego, se articula con el maestro de aula. A través de la plataforma, el maestro puede hacer un seguimiento de lo que se trabaja, pero la institución en sí no cuenta con ese mismo seguimiento.

En cambio, sí puede realizarlo cuando se trata de un profesor presencial, ya que este es un docente más de la escuela.

8. Como profesora de Didáctica, ¿qué opinas sobre la formación en inglés de los estudiantes magisteriales?

Como profesora de Didáctica, valoro la formación de los futuros docentes. Más que centrarme exclusivamente en el inglés, diría que habría que fortalecer los aspectos didácticos relacionados con la enseñanza de una segunda lengua.

Sé que, en el Plan 2008, el estudiante de Magisterio tiene en cuarto año una asignatura de inglés, denominada Lenguas Extranjeras, opción Inglés, si no recuerdo mal. En el Plan 2023 también está contemplada en cuarto año y cuenta con un programa que se desarrolla durante todo el año. Considero que fortalece tanto los aspectos disciplinares como los didácticos, aunque a veces puede no ser suficiente.

Ahora hemos cambiado de plan y no sé si en el Plan 2027 está contemplada o no. De todas maneras, considero que esta formación debería priorizar el uso comunicativo del idioma y las estrategias didácticas que el maestro pueda emplear de manera funcional.

Sin embargo, sabemos que para enseñar inglés se necesita una formación disciplinar muy amplia, que lleva años desarrollar y que nosotros no necesariamente tenemos.

9. Desde tu perspectiva como formadora en Didáctica, ¿qué consejo le darías a un estudiante de Profesorado que está próximo a comenzar esa materia?

Hay una frase que me gusta mucho que dice: Para enseñar latín no solo se debe saber latín, sino que también se debe saber cómo enseñar latín.

El estudiante tiene que trabajar profundamente en esas dos dimensiones: saber mucho inglés y saber cómo se enseña el inglés. Es decir, debe combinar los conocimientos disciplinares con los didácticos. Esa conjugación es fundamental para el éxito del proceso de enseñanza.

Después hay una tercera cuestión que va a surgir a medida que avance en Didáctica y comience a realizar la práctica: la enseñanza situada y el aprendizaje situado. Esto implica considerar qué necesita ese alumno en particular, dentro de una determinada institución, un determinado contexto y un determinado año.

Hay un tercer elemento que debemos integrar: conocer a quién le enseñamos. En definitiva, para enseñar latín hay que saber latín, saber cómo se enseña latín y, además, conocer a quién se le enseña latín. En este caso, estaríamos hablando del inglés.

06 Marisol Fernández Primary teacher · School-based English teaching experience +

1. Para comenzar, ¿podrías contarnos acerca de tu trayectoria profesional y de las experiencias que ha tenido con la enseñanza del inglés en la escuela?

Soy Marisol Fernández, maestra de Educación Primaria, y hace veinte años que me desempeño en escuelas de práctica de la ciudad de Mercedes, Soriano. A lo largo de mi trayectoria he trabajado en doble turno y he tenido la oportunidad de participar en diferentes propuestas educativas.

En relación con la enseñanza del inglés, he tenido experiencias tanto con profesores especializados que trabajan presencialmente en el aula como con propuestas desarrolladas mediante videoconferencias a través de Ceibal.

Estas experiencias me han permitido conocer distintas formas de abordar la enseñanza de una lengua extranjera y reflexionar sobre su importancia en la formación de los niños.

2. Desde tu experiencia, ¿cómo ha sido el trabajo con profesores de inglés que concurren presencialmente a la escuela?

He tenido experiencias muy buenas con profesores de inglés que trabajan presencialmente en el aula. En algunas oportunidades, he compartido el trabajo con profesionales muy comprometidos, con una gran capacidad para generar propuestas motivadoras para los niños.

También he tenido otras experiencias que no han resultado tan significativas. Esto me lleva a considerar que la presencia de un profesor especializado es importante, pero también lo son su formación, sus estrategias didácticas y su capacidad para generar vínculos con los estudiantes.

Personalmente, prefiero la modalidad presencial, ya que permite una interacción más directa, un acompañamiento más cercano y una mayor posibilidad de atender las necesidades que surgen durante el aprendizaje.

3. ¿Qué opinión te merece la enseñanza del inglés mediante videoconferencias, como las propuestas desarrolladas por Ceibal?

He tenido experiencias positivas con las propuestas de Ceibal, aunque también he encontrado algunas dificultades.

Considero que la videoconferencia constituye una oportunidad valiosa para acercar el inglés a las escuelas, especialmente cuando no se cuenta con un profesor especializado que pueda trabajar presencialmente.

Sin embargo, desde el lugar de la maestra de aula, esta modalidad implica asumir determinadas responsabilidades. El profesor remoto desarrolla la clase, pero la maestra debe acompañar a los estudiantes, favorecer la comprensión de las consignas y dar continuidad a las actividades.

En algunas oportunidades, he tenido que recurrir a los aportes del profesor remoto y a personas con formación en el área para poder llevar adelante las actividades de inglés que debía desarrollar como docente de aula.

Por eso, considero que la modalidad virtual ofrece posibilidades importantes, aunque requiere una buena articulación entre el profesor remoto y el maestro que se encuentra en el salón.

4. ¿Qué dificultades encuentra al tener que acompañar o desarrollar propuestas de inglés sin contar con una formación específica en el área?

La principal dificultad es que, como maestras de aula, debemos acompañar procesos de enseñanza y aprendizaje de una lengua extranjera sin necesariamente contar con los conocimientos lingüísticos y didácticos específicos que esta tarea requiere.

Si bien podemos orientar a los estudiantes, acompañar las actividades y favorecer el trabajo colaborativo, enseñar inglés implica mucho más que conocer algunas palabras o expresiones.

Se necesita formación para trabajar aspectos como la pronunciación, la comprensión oral, la comunicación y las diferentes habilidades que intervienen en el aprendizaje de una lengua.

En mi caso, he tenido que recurrir a diferentes apoyos para poder llevar adelante las propuestas. Esto me ha permitido reconocer la importancia de contar con profesionales especializados y de fortalecer la formación de los maestros en esta área.

5. ¿Qué estrategias ha utilizado para favorecer el aprendizaje de los niños cuando debe acompañar las clases de inglés?

Una de las estrategias que he utilizado es el trabajo colaborativo entre los estudiantes.

En determinadas oportunidades, he recurrido a los niños que presentan mayores avances en el aprendizaje para que puedan acompañar a sus compañeros y actuar como monitores durante las actividades.

Esta dinámica favorece el intercambio, permite aprovechar los conocimientos que circulan dentro del grupo y genera nuevas oportunidades de aprendizaje.

De todas maneras, considero que el trabajo entre pares debe ser una estrategia de apoyo y no una sustitución del rol docente. Es importante que todos los niños puedan participar y que cada uno tenga la oportunidad de avanzar desde sus propias posibilidades.

6. ¿Considera que los maestros deberían recibir formación específica en inglés durante su formación inicial o a través de instancias de formación permanente?

Sí, considero que es una formación necesaria.

Si bien no todos los maestros debemos convertirnos en especialistas en la enseñanza del inglés, es importante contar con herramientas que nos permitan comprender las propuestas, acompañar los procesos de aprendizaje y desarrollar las actividades con mayor seguridad.

Ceibal ofrece cursos de inglés dirigidos a maestros. Si bien actualmente considero que realizar esta formación representa para mí un gran desafío, tampoco la descarto.

Creo que la formación continua es una responsabilidad profesional y una oportunidad para seguir aprendiendo. Una propuesta de formación en inglés podría contribuir a fortalecer nuestras prácticas y mejorar las experiencias que ofrecemos a los estudiantes.

7. Desde tu perspectiva, ¿qué importancia tiene el aprendizaje del inglés para los niños de Educación Primaria?

Considero que el inglés es una herramienta fundamental para la formación de los niños en el mundo actual.

Vivimos en una sociedad cada vez más globalizada, en la que la comunicación, el acceso a la información y las posibilidades de intercambio con otras culturas adquieren una importancia creciente.

El inglés es una puerta al mundo. Permite acceder a nuevos conocimientos, comunicarse con personas de diferentes lugares, comprender otras realidades y ampliar las posibilidades de participación social, cultural y académica, así como las oportunidades laborales en el futuro.

Por este motivo, considero que todos los niños deberían tener la oportunidad de aprender inglés, independientemente de su contexto social o de sus posibilidades de acceder a propuestas educativas fuera de la escuela.

La educación pública tiene un papel fundamental en este sentido, ya que puede contribuir a democratizar el acceso a una formación que resulta cada vez más necesaria.

8. ¿Qué aspectos consideras fundamentales para que exista una buena articulación entre el profesor de inglés y el maestro de aula?

Considero que el diálogo, la planificación compartida y el conocimiento de las características del grupo son aspectos fundamentales.

El maestro de aula conoce las dinámicas del grupo, las necesidades de los estudiantes y sus procesos de aprendizaje. Por su parte, el profesor de inglés cuenta con los conocimientos específicos de la lengua y su didáctica.

Cuando ambos profesionales pueden trabajar de manera articulada, se generan mejores condiciones para acompañar a los niños.

También considero importante que exista una distribución clara de las responsabilidades, de manera que el maestro pueda colaborar sin tener que asumir tareas para las cuales no cuenta con la formación necesaria.

9. ¿Qué condiciones consideras necesarias para mejorar la enseñanza del inglés en las escuelas públicas?

Considero que es necesario fortalecer la presencia de profesores especializados, promover el trabajo colaborativo entre los profesores de inglés y los maestros de aula, y ofrecer instancias de formación continua que respondan a las necesidades reales de los docentes.

También resulta importante contar con recursos didácticos adecuados, propuestas motivadoras y condiciones institucionales que permitan desarrollar las clases de manera significativa.

Desde mi experiencia, el compromiso de los docentes es fundamental, pero debe estar acompañado por una formación pertinente y por políticas educativas que garanticen oportunidades de aprendizaje para todos los estudiantes.

10. Para finalizar, ¿qué reflexión le gustaría compartir con un futuro profesor de inglés que se está formando en el IFD?

Le diría que la enseñanza del inglés implica una gran responsabilidad, porque estamos ofreciendo a los niños una herramienta que puede ampliar sus posibilidades de comunicación, de acceso al conocimiento y de comprensión del mundo.

Desde mi experiencia como maestra, considero fundamental que todos los niños tengan la oportunidad de acercarse a esta lengua desde la escuela, mediante propuestas de calidad y con docentes preparados para acompañar sus aprendizajes.

También le transmitiría la importancia de trabajar en equipo con los maestros de aula, conocer las características de los grupos y comprender que cada niño aprende de una manera diferente.

Estoy convencida de que el inglés constituye una puerta al mundo y que, en una sociedad cada vez más globalizada, su enseñanza debe ocupar un lugar relevante en la educación de todos los niños.
