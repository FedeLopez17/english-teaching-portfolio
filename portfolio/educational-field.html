National context

The Uruguayan Educational System

The Uruguayan education system is an institutional, pedagogical and social structure intended to guarantee the right to education and promote people's comprehensive development throughout their lives. Its current organisation is the result of a long historical process characterised by the consolidation of public education, the establishment of a legal framework based on the principles of secularism, free education and compulsory attendance, and successive changes in educational policies and curricula.

This report examines the organisation of the national education system, its main transformations and the different institutions that make up its educational provision. It also places this information within the context of the department of Soriano, identifying its educational institutions, community actors and available learning opportunities.

Special attention is given to English language teaching, including its presence in public and private education, students' learning opportunities, teacher education and possible employment opportunities in the department.

Finally, the report explores the main curricular documents established by the Administración Nacional de Educación Pública (ANEP), particularly the National Curriculum Framework (Marco Curricular Nacional), Integrated Basic Education (Educación Básica Integrada) and the guidelines for foreign language teaching. These documents provide an important reference for understanding the pedagogical and teaching decisions that guide educational practice.

Institutional Organisation and Legal Framework

The Uruguayan education system involves different public and private institutions responsible for managing, regulating and providing education at various levels.

Public non-university education is mainly administered by the Administración Nacional de Educación Pública (ANEP), an autonomous organisation whose structure includes the Consejo Directivo Central (CODICEN) and several directorates and councils.

Its main bodies include:

  • Dirección General de Educación Inicial y Primaria (DGEIP): responsible for early childhood and primary education.
  • Dirección General de Educación Secundaria (DGES): responsible for secondary education.
  • Dirección General de Educación Técnico Profesional (DGETP-UTU): provides technical, technological and vocational education.
  • Consejo de Formación en Educación (CFE): responsible for initial teacher education and other professional development opportunities for educators.

The Ministerio de Educación y Cultura (MEC) is responsible for coordinating and guiding national educational policies. It also participates in areas such as non-formal education and the regulation of private tertiary education institutions, according to its legal responsibilities.

The Universidad de la República (Udelar) and the Universidad Tecnológica (UTEC) are part of Uruguay's public higher education system and have institutional autonomy.

From a legal perspective, the General Education Law (Ley General de Educación N.º 18.437), enacted in 2008, is a fundamental reference. It recognises education as a fundamental human right and a public and social good, establishing the State's responsibility to guarantee the conditions necessary for its exercise.

The system is also based on three historical principles of Uruguayan public education: laicidad, gratuidad and obligatoriedad (secularism, free education and compulsory education).

Educational Levels and Types of Institutions

Formal education in Uruguay is organised into different levels and modalities, aiming to ensure continuity throughout students' educational trajectories, from early childhood to higher education.

The main levels and types of institutions are:

Educational level Types of institutions Main organisations
Early Childhood Education Kindergartens and early childhood education centres DGEIP, INAU and private institutions
Primary Education Urban, rural, special, practice, full-time and extended-time schools DGEIP
Lower Secondary Education Secondary schools (liceos) and technical schools DGES and DGETP
Upper Secondary Education Secondary schools, technical schools and vocational training centres DGES and DGETP
Tertiary Education Teacher education institutes and technical and technological institutes CFE and DGETP
University Education Faculties, university centres and institutes Udelar, UTEC and private universities

This institutional diversity responds to different educational needs. While some institutions focus on general academic education, others offer technical, vocational or specialised programmes.

According to ANEP's 2024 data, the public education system had 2,842 educational establishments, including kindergartens, schools, secondary schools, technical schools and teacher education centres (ANEP, 2025).

Private education also forms part of the system. Depending on the educational level and applicable regulations, it includes early childhood centres, schools, secondary schools, bilingual institutions and language institutes.

Main Educational Transformations and Reforms in Uruguay

The history of Uruguayan education includes several reforms intended to respond to social, cultural, economic and pedagogical changes.

One of the most significant historical developments was the school reform promoted by José Pedro Varela in 1876. It contributed to establishing a public education model based on secularism, free education and compulsory attendance.

In more recent times, the General Education Law N.º 18.437, enacted in 2008, established an important legal framework by recognising education as a human right and defining principles for the organisation of the National Education System.

Later, between 2020 and 2024, ANEP implemented the Transformación Curricular Integral, within the context of the changes introduced by Law N.º 19.889, commonly known as the Ley de Urgente Consideración.

This transformation introduced changes to curricular organisation and to the way teaching and learning were understood. Its main components included:

  • National Curriculum Framework (MCN): establishes the guiding principles, general competencies and expected graduation profile for compulsory education.
  • Integrated Basic Education (EBI): reorganises compulsory education from early childhood education to ninth grade through three cycles and six tramos.
  • Learning progressions (progresiones de aprendizaje): describe how competencies develop throughout students' educational trajectories.
  • Stage profiles (perfiles de tramo): establish expected achievements at the end of each tramo.
  • New curricular programmes: connect specific competencies, contents and teaching guidelines.
  • Formative assessment: promotes the monitoring of learning processes and the use of feedback.

The transformation aimed to strengthen educational continuity, student-centred learning, interdisciplinary work and competency development. However, its implementation also generated debate regarding teacher participation, implementation times, professional development and the relationship between disciplinary knowledge and competency-based education.

Since 2025, educational policies and curricular programmes have continued to undergo review and updating. It is therefore important to distinguish the main structural documents of the curriculum from subsequent modifications to specific programmes. The publication of updated curricular documents and 2026 course structures reflects the continuation of these processes (ANEP, DGES, 2026).

Digital Inclusion and Educational Innovation Policies

The incorporation of digital technologies is another important aspect of Uruguayan educational policies. Plan Ceibal has contributed to expanding access to devices, internet connectivity and digital resources in public educational institutions.

Its work goes beyond providing equipment. It also includes educational platforms, teaching resources, teacher training and pedagogical innovation initiatives.

In foreign language education, technology has made it possible to develop distance-learning experiences and communication activities through videoconferencing.

These initiatives are particularly relevant in rural schools, where geographical distance and the availability of specialised teachers can make it difficult to provide continuous access to certain educational opportunities.

Structural Challenges of the Education System

Despite the expansion of educational coverage and the implementation of curricular reforms, the Uruguayan education system still faces challenges related to students' participation, continuity and completion of their educational trajectories.

Secondary education is particularly relevant in this discussion because inequalities in graduation rates persist among different social groups.

Some of the main challenges include:

  • Reducing inequalities associated with students' socioeconomic backgrounds.
  • Strengthening continuity between primary and secondary education.
  • Reducing grade repetition and educational disengagement.
  • Improving institutional conditions to address student diversity.
  • Guaranteeing learning opportunities in both urban and rural contexts.
  • Strengthening teacher education and professional support.

Addressing these challenges requires sustained policies that consider the curricular, institutional and social dimensions of education.

English Language Teaching in the National Education System

English Language Teaching in the National Education System

English language teaching is an important part of Uruguay's educational policies because of its role in communication, access to knowledge, cultural exchange and participation in academic and professional environments.

In public primary education, English is taught through different programmes and modalities, including:

  • Face-to-face teaching through second and foreign language programmes.
  • Ceibal en Inglés, which incorporates technology-mediated teaching and videoconferencing.
  • Inglés sin Límites, aimed at rural schools and certain institutions that do not have access to other English teaching modalities.

Inglés sin Límites is a particular initiative because it uses video lessons and teaching materials that allow classroom teachers to develop English activities without requiring a permanent internet connection.

In secondary education, English is part of the curricular offer of both DGES and DGETP. Within Integrated Basic Education, it is included in the lower secondary years, while in upper secondary education it follows the corresponding curricular programmes and guidelines.

Private institutions offer different approaches to English teaching. Some include it as a regular subject, while others follow bilingual models in which English is also used to teach content from other areas.

These differences create varying opportunities for students to interact with the language. Therefore, the presence of English in the curriculum does not necessarily mean that all students receive the same amount of exposure or develop equivalent communicative skills.

In this context, the Uruguay Plurilingüe 2030 policy establishes the goal of enabling students in public secondary education to reach B2 of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) by the end of their studies.

English Teacher Education in Uruguay

English teacher education is essential for ensuring the quality and continuity of language teaching in the country.

In the public sector, specialised teacher education is mainly provided by the Consejo de Formación en Educación (CFE). Its programmes combine linguistic, pedagogical, didactic and disciplinary knowledge.

The English teaching degree, under Plan 2023, lasts four years. Its curriculum includes the development of communicative competence in English, linguistic knowledge, specific teaching methodology, educational sciences and professional practice.

In addition to full teacher education programmes, there are also certification programmes for teaching English in public primary schools. These are aimed at applicants with a minimum English proficiency equivalent to B2 of the CEFR and focus on developing the teaching skills needed for primary education.

It is important to distinguish these certifications from the four-year English teacher education degree, since their admission requirements, duration and professional scope are different.

Ultimately, becoming an English teacher involves more than mastering the language. It also requires the ability to design appropriate learning experiences, respond to student diversity and promote meaningful learning.

02

Local context

Educational Community of Soriano

The department of Soriano has an educational network distributed across urban and rural areas, including public and private institutions that provide different levels and types of education.

Mercedes, as the departmental capital, concentrates a significant proportion of educational, administrative and teacher education services. However, other localities, such as Dolores, Cardona, Palmitas, José Enrique Rodó and Villa Soriano, also have institutions that play an important role in their communities.

The distribution of educational centres reflects the department's demographic, social and productive characteristics, which include agricultural, commercial, industrial and service activities.

Early Childhood and Primary Education

Public early childhood and primary education is managed by the Dirección General de Educación Inicial y Primaria (DGEIP). Its institutional network includes urban and rural schools, kindergartens and centres with different approaches to extending learning time.

These include regular schools, rural schools, special education schools, full-time schools and extended-time schools, as well as institutions that develop specific inclusion and student support initiatives.

The Soriano Departmental Inspectorate of Early Childhood and Primary Education, based in Mercedes, plays an important role in coordinating and supervising these institutions.

Secondary Education

Public secondary education is organised through two main subsystems:

  • Dirección General de Educación Secundaria (DGES): manages public secondary schools (liceos) in the department. In Mercedes, these include:
    • Liceo N.º 1 Departamental "José María Campos".
    • Liceo N.º 2 "Luis Alberto Zanzi".
    • Liceo N.º 3 "Prof. Gregorio Cardozo Loyarte".
    • Liceo N.º 4.
    • The department also has secondary schools in Dolores, Cardona, Palmitas, Palmar and José Enrique Rodó, among other localities.
  • Dirección General de Educación Técnico Profesional (DGETP-UTU): provides technical, technological and vocational education. In Mercedes, its institutions include:
    • Escuela Técnica de Mercedes "Washington Lockhart", located on General Artigas 189.
    • Escuela Técnica Superior "Pedro Blanes Viale", located on Víctor de Haedo.
    • Other UTU centres operate in localities such as Dolores, Cardona, Villa Soriano and Colonia Concordia.

The coexistence of these two subsystems provides students with different alternatives for continuing their education, whether through general academic or technical-professional pathways.

Tertiary and Higher Education

Soriano's tertiary education offer includes institutions and programmes related to teacher education, technical education and university studies.

In Mercedes, the Instituto de Formación Docente “Maestro Mario A. López Thode” operates under the Consejo de Formación en Educación. It is an important institution for initial teacher education, including primary teacher education and other educational programmes.

The department also has university-level opportunities. UTEC offers the Licenciatura en Jazz y Música Creativa at its Instituto Tecnológico Regional Suroeste. Udelar has also offered programmes in the department, including the Tecnólogo en Administración y Contabilidad.

In addition, regional centres of UTEC and Udelar in nearby departments provide further opportunities for students from Soriano. It is important, however, to distinguish between institutions physically located in Soriano and those that are accessible through regional educational networks.

Some figures help illustrate the scale of this provision. According to data from the MEC's 2024 Statistical Yearbook, Soriano had 614 enrolments in public tertiary programmes taught exclusively in the department: 553 in teacher education and 61 at UTEC. In addition, UTU had 312 students enrolled in non-university tertiary courses in the department. These figures refer to different educational categories and should not be treated as a single university enrolment total (MEC, 2026).

At upper secondary level, the same yearbook recorded 935 students in the final year of public secondary schools and 1,591 students enrolled in upper secondary UTU programmes. These figures illustrate the importance of both general and technical education in the department.

Private Educational Institutions

Private education is also part of Soriano's educational network. In Mercedes, two examples are:

  • Uruguayan American School (UAS) Mercedes: offers a bilingual educational proposal and identifies itself as a Cambridge International School. Its provision includes Early Years and Primary Education.
  • Colegio y Liceo San Miguel: a private educational institution located on Artigas 500 in Mercedes.

These institutions contribute to the diversity of educational opportunities in the department and represent different contexts in which teachers may work.

Educational Community Actors and Institutional Cooperation

An educational community is not limited to schools and other educational institutions. It also includes the different social actors who participate directly or indirectly in teaching and learning processes.

In Soriano, these actors include:

  • Management and supervision bodies: ANEP, departmental and regional inspectorates, and school management teams.
  • Educational professionals: teachers, primary school teachers, secondary school teachers, school administrators, adscriptos and support staff.
  • Students and families: central participants in educational trajectories and institutional life.
  • Teacher education and higher education institutions: the IFD, UTU centres, Udelar and regional higher education institutions.
  • Social and cultural institutions: libraries, museums, community organisations and cultural spaces.
  • The productive sector: agricultural, industrial, commercial, logistics and service companies.

Cooperation among these actors can strengthen the relationship between educational institutions and their communities. It also makes it possible to develop learning experiences that respond more closely to the social and cultural characteristics of the department.

03

Language education

English Language Teaching in Soriano

English teaching in Soriano varies according to the type of institution, the locality and the opportunities available to students.

In public education, English is taught within ANEP's national language policies through face-to-face programmes, technology-mediated proposals and initiatives specifically designed for rural schools.

In private education, the offer includes institutions with English as a curricular subject, bilingual educational proposals and specialised language institutes.

Private schools

In Mercedes, several institutions provide opportunities for learning English.

Private schools contribute to the diversity of educational opportunities available in Mercedes. Their proposals differ in terms of curricular organisation and the importance given to foreign language learning.

  • Uruguayan American School (UAS) Mercedes: is a private institution that offers a bilingual educational proposal based on the integration of Spanish and English. It is also registered as a Cambridge International School. English is an important part of its educational approach and is incorporated into students' learning experiences from the early years.

    In Early Years, children are introduced to English through everyday activities such as songs, games, stories, conversations and routines. In Primary Education, the proposal becomes more extensive. Students work with the Uruguayan primary curriculum in Spanish during the morning and continue their education in English during the afternoon. In addition to specific English lessons, they study areas such as Science, Humanities and Global Perspectives through English. This allows them to use the language not only to communicate but also to explore topics, develop ideas and acquire knowledge.

    This approach provides sustained exposure to English and illustrates how bilingual education can involve learning academic content through a second language. The institution's current educational offer includes Early Years and Primary Education.

  • Colegio y Liceo San Miguel: is another private educational institution in Mercedes. It contributes to the department's educational provision and offers families an alternative to public education. Its specific English programme, however, should be considered separately from UAS's bilingual proposal, since the inclusion of English as a school subject does not necessarily imply that other areas are taught through the language.

Language Institutes

Mercedes also has several institutions dedicated specifically to foreign language teaching. They offer opportunities for children, teenagers and adults to begin learning English, improve their proficiency, receive academic support or prepare for further educational and professional goals.

  • Instituto Cultural Anglo-Uruguayo (Anglo): is part of one of Uruguay's best-known networks for English language education. It has a centre in Mercedes and another in Cardona. The Anglo network offers courses for different age groups and proficiency levels, providing a structured learning pathway that can accompany students over several years. Its broader educational network is also associated with international English language examinations, particularly those offered by Cambridge.
  • Alianza: the Alianza Cultural Uruguay–Estados Unidos has a branch in Mercedes. It is a non-profit cultural and educational organisation whose activities include English language teaching and the promotion of cultural exchange. Its courses cater to different levels and age groups, making it an additional option for people interested in learning English outside formal schooling.
  • Dickens: has an institute in Mercedes and offers English courses for different age groups and educational needs. In addition to general language instruction, it provides teacher development opportunities, including courses for English teachers. This makes it relevant not only to language learners but also to educators interested in continuing their professional development.

English Teacher Education in Soriano

Teacher education is an important factor when considering the development of English language teaching in the department.

Mercedes has the Instituto de Formación Docente “Maestro Mario A. López Thode”, which belongs to the Consejo de Formación en Educación. However, the existence of this institution does not necessarily mean that every teaching specialisation is offered there in person every academic year.

The English teacher education degree follows CFE's Plan 2023 and lasts four years. It combines language development, linguistics, specific teaching methodology, educational sciences and professional practice.

For students living outside Montevideo, semi-presential study options are particularly important because they can provide access to programmes that are not available in their own localities.

In the case of Soriano, the specific locations and modalities available for studying English teaching should be checked against the CFE's enrolment offer for each academic year. The general existence of a teacher education institution in Mercedes should not be confused with the availability of every specialisation there.

Teacher education also requires more than linguistic proficiency. Future teachers need to develop the ability to plan lessons, assess learning and adapt their proposals to different students and educational contexts.

04

Professional outlook

Employment Opportunities and Demand for English Teachers

Employment opportunities for English teachers in Soriano are mainly connected to three areas: public education, private institutions and independent teaching.

In public education, possible workplaces include secondary schools, technical schools and language teaching programmes. Recruitment calls for teaching hours can provide an indication of institutional needs, although their existence alone does not prove a permanent shortage of qualified teachers.

In the private sector, schools, bilingual institutions and language institutes offer additional possibilities.

In Mercedes, potential workplaces include UAS Mercedes, Colegio y Liceo San Miguel, Anglo, Alianza and Dickens. These institutions represent different teaching contexts, ranging from formal school education to specialised language courses.

Independent teaching is another possibility. Teachers may offer private lessons, academic support, exam preparation and conversation courses.

English proficiency can also be valuable in a department with productive and commercial activities related to agriculture, industry, transport, logistics, tourism and services. However, it is important to distinguish between the general demand for English proficiency in these sectors and the specific demand for English teachers. They are related but different employment areas.

Overall, the available information suggests that there are several possible professional pathways for English teachers in Soriano. Nevertheless, there are not enough departmental statistics to establish the exact number of available positions or compare the number of graduates with the demand for teachers.

The Role of These Institutions in the Local Educational Community

Together, private schools and language institutes expand the English learning opportunities available in Mercedes. They also represent potential workplaces for future English teachers, who may encounter different student profiles, institutional expectations and teaching objectives.

These opportunities are relevant to the wider educational community of Soriano, as they allow students and families to choose among different proposals according to their interests and needs. However, access may depend on factors such as location, course availability and families' financial circumstances. The presence of private alternatives therefore complements, rather than replaces, the responsibility of public education to guarantee access to foreign language learning.

Considerations on the Department's Educational Reality

The educational community of Soriano is characterised by a diverse network of institutions distributed across urban and rural areas. Schools, secondary schools, UTU centres, private institutions and teacher education centres all contribute to the department's educational provision.

The figures for secondary and tertiary education also show that students have access to different pathways, although the availability of programmes and institutions is not the same in every locality.

In English language teaching, public programmes, private schools and language institutes provide different opportunities for learning. At the same time, challenges remain regarding continuity, the availability of specialised teachers and differences between urban and rural contexts.

For someone preparing to become an English teacher in Soriano, this local context is particularly relevant. Public education, private schools and language institutes represent different possible workplaces, each with its own students, institutional expectations and teaching conditions. Understanding these differences is an important first step towards entering the profession.

Strengthening teacher education and cooperation among institutions can contribute to expanding learning opportunities and improving the educational offer throughout the department.

Professional Organisations

Professional Organisations

Two relevant organisations in the field of English language teaching in Uruguay are:

  • AUDEPPi (Asociación Uruguaya de Profesores Particulares de Inglés): founded in 1992, it brings together private English teachers and promotes professional development, academic training and teacher updating. It is a professional association, not a language institute.
  • URUTESOL: founded in 1988, it brings together English teachers from both public and private education. It is affiliated with TESOL and associated with IATEFL, promoting professional standards, research and exchange among teachers at national and international levels.

Although these organisations are not language institutes based in Soriano, they are relevant to English teachers working in the department because they provide opportunities for professional development and networking.
