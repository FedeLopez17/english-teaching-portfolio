Reference

Key Concepts & Glossary

A collection of terms and concepts related to education, language learning and classroom practice.

01

The Uruguayan Educational System

ANEP – Administración Nacional de Educación Pública
The public institution responsible for governing and administering Uruguay's public education system, including initial, primary, secondary and technical-professional education.
CODICEN – Consejo Directivo Central
The central governing body of ANEP, responsible for coordinating and making decisions about the public education system.
DGEIP – Dirección General de Educación Inicial y Primaria
The ANEP directorate responsible for managing public initial and primary education.
DGES – Dirección General de Educación Secundaria
The ANEP directorate responsible for managing public secondary education.
DGETP – Dirección General de Educación Técnico Profesional
The ANEP directorate responsible for managing technical and professional education.
UTU – Universidad del Trabajo del Uruguay
The institution traditionally associated with technical and vocational education in Uruguay, commonly referred to as DGETP-UTU.
EBI – Educación Básica Integrada
The educational structure that brings together initial, primary and lower secondary education into a continuous basic education pathway.
EMS – Educación Media Superior
The stage of education that follows basic education and offers different educational pathways.
MCN – Marco Curricular Nacional
The national framework that establishes the principles, competencies and guidelines for curriculum development in Uruguay.
CFE – Consejo de Formación en Educación
The institution responsible for the initial training of teachers and other education professionals in Uruguay.
IFD – Instituto de Formación Docente
An institution that provides initial teacher training, mainly for initial and primary education.
CERP – Centro Regional de Profesores
A type of regional institution that provides initial teacher training, particularly for secondary education.
IPA – Instituto de Profesores Artigas
A teacher education institution in Montevideo that offers initial training for secondary school teachers.
INEEd – Instituto Nacional de Evaluación Educativa
An independent institution responsible for evaluating the quality of education in Uruguay and producing information to support its improvement.
02

Second-Language Acquisition and English Language Teaching

SLA – Second Language Acquisition
The process through which people develop the ability to understand and use a language other than their first language.
L1 – First Language
The first language a person acquires, usually during childhood.
L2 – Second Language
A language acquired or learned after a person's first language. The term can also refer more generally to any additional language.
EFL – English as a Foreign Language
The learning and teaching of English in contexts where it is not the main language used in everyday communication.
ESL – English as a Second Language
The learning and teaching of English in contexts where it is widely used in everyday communication.
ELT – English Language Teaching
The field concerned with the teaching and learning of English.
TEFL – Teaching English as a Foreign Language
The practice of teaching English in contexts where it is not the main language spoken in society.
TESOL – Teaching English to Speakers of Other Languages
The practice of teaching English to people whose first language is not English, including both EFL and ESL contexts.
CEFR – Common European Framework of Reference for Languages
An international framework used to describe and compare language proficiency through six levels, from A1 to C2.
A1 – Beginner
Can understand and use basic everyday expressions, introduce themselves and ask simple questions.
A2 – Elementary
Can communicate in simple, routine situations and understand frequently used expressions about familiar topics.
B1 – Intermediate
Can understand the main points of clear speech and texts, handle most situations while travelling and produce simple connected texts.
B2 – Upper-Intermediate
Can understand more complex texts and discussions, interact with a degree of fluency and express opinions on a wide range of topics.
C1 – Advanced
Can understand demanding texts, recognise implicit meaning and express themselves fluently and flexibly in different contexts.
C2 – Proficient
Can understand virtually everything they hear or read and express themselves spontaneously, precisely and effectively, even in complex situations.
Language Acquisition vs. Language Learning
A distinction often associated with Stephen Krashen. Acquisition refers to developing language ability through meaningful exposure and communication, while learning involves consciously studying language rules and structures.
Comprehensible Input
Language input that learners can understand, even when it contains some unfamiliar words or structures, allowing them to develop their language skills.
Input Hypothesis
Stephen Krashen's hypothesis that language acquisition occurs when learners are exposed to comprehensible input slightly above their current level of proficiency, often represented as i + 1.
Output Hypothesis
Merrill Swain's hypothesis that producing language helps learners notice gaps in their knowledge, test their language hypotheses and refine their language use.
Interaction Hypothesis
Michael Long's hypothesis that interaction supports language acquisition, especially when learners negotiate meaning, ask for clarification and receive feedback.
Interlanguage
The developing language system created by learners as they acquire a new language. It has its own rules and changes as learners progress.
Language Transfer
The influence of a person's first language on their use or learning of another language, which can either facilitate learning or lead to errors.
Affective Filter
A concept proposed by Stephen Krashen suggesting that emotions such as anxiety, lack of confidence and low motivation can create barriers to language acquisition.
ZPD – Zone of Proximal Development
A concept developed by Lev Vygotsky that refers to the difference between what a learner can do independently and what they can achieve with guidance or support.
TTT – Teacher Talking Time
The amount of time a teacher spends speaking during a lesson.
STT – Student Talking Time
The amount of time learners spend speaking during a lesson, giving them opportunities to practise and develop their communication skills.
03

Language, Communication and Linguistics

Code-Switching
The practice of alternating between two or more languages or language varieties during a conversation or interaction.
Translanguaging
An approach that recognises how multilingual speakers use their entire linguistic repertoire to communicate and learn, rather than treating each language as completely separate.
Communicative Competence
The ability to use language effectively and appropriately in different situations, combining grammatical knowledge with an understanding of social contexts and communication strategies.
Sociolinguistic Competence
The ability to use language appropriately according to the social context, including the relationship between speakers, their roles and the situation.
Intelligibility
The degree to which a speaker's message can be understood by others, focusing on effective communication rather than having a native-like accent.
04

Teaching Approaches and Methodology

CLT – Communicative Language Teaching
A language teaching approach that focuses on developing learners' ability to communicate through meaningful interaction and real-life situations.
TBLT – Task-Based Language Teaching
A language teaching approach in which learners develop their language skills by completing meaningful tasks, such as solving problems or exchanging information.
PPP – Presentation, Practice, Production
A lesson framework in which the teacher first presents a language point, learners practise it through guided activities and then use it more independently.
Lesson Plan
A document that outlines the learning objectives, activities, materials, timing and assessment methods for a lesson.
Elicitation
A teaching technique in which the teacher encourages learners to provide answers, ideas or language instead of simply giving them the information.
05

Teaching and Classroom Practice

Scaffolding
The temporary support provided by a teacher or another person to help learners complete tasks or understand concepts they cannot manage independently. This support is gradually reduced as learners become more capable.
Classroom Management
The strategies teachers use to organise the classroom, establish expectations, manage behaviour and create an environment that supports learning.
Physical Presence
The way a teacher uses their body, position, movement and physical behaviour to interact with learners and influence the classroom environment.
Proximity
The physical distance a teacher maintains from learners during a lesson, which can influence interaction, comfort and classroom management.
Appropriacy
The ability to choose behaviours, actions and ways of interacting that are suitable for the learners, context and situation.
Voice Modulation
The variation of a teacher's tone, pitch and volume to maintain learners' attention and communicate different meanings or emotions.
Voice Projection
The ability to make one's voice clearly audible throughout the classroom without having to shout.
Classroom Climate
The general atmosphere of a classroom, including whether learners feel safe, respected, comfortable and supported.
Rapport
A positive relationship between teachers and learners, built through trust, mutual respect, understanding and effective communication.
Student-Centred Learning
A teaching approach that places learners' needs, interests and active participation at the centre of the learning process.
Differentiation
The adaptation of teaching methods, materials, activities or assessment to respond to learners' different abilities, needs and learning preferences.
Inclusive Education
An educational approach that aims to ensure all learners can participate meaningfully in education, regardless of their backgrounds, abilities or individual needs.
DUA – Diseño Universal para el Aprendizaje
An educational framework that promotes flexible learning environments by offering different ways for learners to engage with content, access information and demonstrate what they have learned.
Collaborative Learning
A teaching approach in which learners work together to achieve shared goals, solve problems or complete activities.
Active Learning
A teaching approach that involves learners directly in the learning process through activities such as discussions, problem-solving, practical tasks and reflection.
Project-Based Learning (PBL)
A teaching approach in which learners develop knowledge and skills by working on a project over an extended period, usually involving research, problem-solving and the creation of a final product.
Game-Based Learning
A teaching approach that uses games as learning tools to help learners develop knowledge and skills through play.
Gamification
The use of game elements, such as points, challenges, levels and rewards, in non-game learning contexts to increase motivation and engagement.
Learner Autonomy
The ability and willingness of learners to take responsibility for their own learning, make decisions and monitor their progress.
Metacognition
The ability to reflect on one's own thinking and learning processes, including planning, monitoring and evaluating progress.
Reflective Practice
The process through which teachers examine and evaluate their own experiences, decisions and teaching methods to improve their professional practice.
CPD – Continuing Professional Development
The ongoing process through which teachers develop their professional knowledge and skills throughout their careers through activities such as courses, workshops, reading, collaboration and reflection.
06

Classroom Organisation and Student Groupings

Lesson Stages
The different parts of a lesson, usually including its opening, main activities and closure, which help organise the learning process.
Lesson Opening
The initial stage of a lesson, during which the teacher introduces the topic, captures learners' attention and prepares them for the activities ahead.
Group Work
A classroom arrangement in which learners work together in small groups to complete a task, solve a problem or achieve a shared goal.
Pair Work
A classroom arrangement in which learners work in pairs to practise language, exchange ideas or complete an activity.
Whole-Class Work
A classroom arrangement in which the teacher works with all learners at the same time, usually for explanations, discussions or shared activities.
Individual Work
A classroom arrangement in which learners complete tasks individually, allowing them to work at their own pace and focus on their own learning.
Peer Observation
The process through which teachers observe each other's lessons to exchange feedback, share ideas and identify ways to improve their teaching practice.
07

Assessment and Evaluation

Assessment
The process of gathering information about learners' knowledge, skills and progress to understand their learning and make educational decisions.
Evaluation
The process of analysing information and making judgements about the quality, effectiveness or results of a learning process, programme or educational activity.
Formative Assessment
Assessment carried out during the learning process to identify learners' progress and difficulties and provide feedback to help them improve.
Summative Assessment
Assessment carried out at the end of a unit, course or learning period to measure learners' overall achievement.
Diagnostic Assessment
Assessment carried out before or at the beginning of a learning process to identify learners' prior knowledge, strengths, needs and possible difficulties.
Self-Assessment
The process through which learners evaluate their own work, knowledge or progress and identify areas for improvement.
Peer Assessment
The process through which learners evaluate and provide feedback on each other's work.
Learning Outcomes
Statements that describe what learners are expected to know, understand or be able to do after completing a learning experience.
Rubric
An assessment tool that describes the criteria used to evaluate a task or piece of work, often including different levels of achievement.
08

Curriculum and Educational Planning

Curriculum
The overall plan for learning in an educational programme, including its objectives, content, competencies, teaching approaches and assessment.
Competency-Based Education
An educational approach that focuses on developing learners' ability to apply knowledge, skills and attitudes to different situations.
Key Competencies
Essential abilities that learners are expected to develop throughout their education, such as communication, critical thinking, problem-solving and collaboration.
Interdisciplinary Learning
A teaching approach that connects knowledge and skills from different subjects to explore topics or solve problems from multiple perspectives.
Hidden Curriculum
The values, attitudes, behaviours and social expectations that learners acquire indirectly through school culture, routines and interactions rather than through the official curriculum.
09

Technology in Education

TIC – Tecnologías de la Información y la Comunicación
Digital technologies and tools used to access, create, store and share information and communicate with others.
CALL – Computer-Assisted Language Learning
The use of computers and digital software to support language teaching and learning through activities such as exercises, simulations and interactive resources.
MALL – Mobile-Assisted Language Learning
The use of mobile devices, such as smartphones and tablets, to support language learning inside or outside the classroom.
Blended Learning
A teaching approach that combines face-to-face instruction with online learning activities and resources.
Flipped Classroom
A teaching approach in which learners study new content before class, often through videos or readings, while classroom time is used for practice, discussion and interactive activities.
